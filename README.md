# Khaurvynzeth

> A mirror of salt reflects the throne of something still beneath the sand.

## The Market at the Bottom of Noon

The market opens when the sun reaches the bottom of the well. Merchants unfold their awnings beneath the sand, and the desert above remains empty except for a single pole carrying a strip of blue cloth.

Travelers discover the entrance by following the cloth's shadow. The shadow descends through the ground as if the sand were clear water. Those who follow it arrive with dry shoes and the taste of oranges in their mouths.

At the far end of the market, an old woman sells mirrors made of salt. Each stands in a frame carved from dark horn. She covers them with linen until a buyer offers a question that has never been asked aloud.

She does not require an answer. An answer would change the price.

## The Buyer With an Empty Portrait

A young man arrived carrying a portrait wrapped in carpet. Its painted room contained a chair, a window, and a vase of white flowers. The person who should have occupied the chair was absent, though a hand remained resting on its arm.

He asked the old woman whether a reflection could remember someone a painting had forgotten. She uncovered the smallest mirror. Its surface showed a staircase leading down through red stone. At its foot stood a chair identical to the painted one.

The young man looked behind himself and found only the market. Looking into the mirror again, he saw the painted hand move from the chair's arm to its seat.

The woman asked him to leave the portrait facing the glass. As the two surfaces met, the flowers in the painted vase bent toward an unseen wind. Outside, high above the market, the blue cloth tightened around its pole.

## A Reflection Too Heavy to Lift

By evening, the small mirror had grown too heavy to move. Its horn frame rested on the counter, but the wood beneath it sagged as if supporting a mountain. The old woman slid seven orange seeds under its lower edge.

The mirror rose slightly. Beneath it, the young man saw a pale claw pressing upward through the counter. Its scales carried fine lines resembling streets seen from a great height. One street ended at the house in the portrait.

He recognized the roof, then the window, then the narrow garden where a tree had never borne fruit. He remembered waiting there for someone whose footsteps always stopped on the other side of the wall.

The claw withdrew. Six seeds had become small stones. The seventh opened into a flower with a center of clear salt.

## The Throne Under the Dunes

When the market lamps were extinguished, the mirror began to shine. The staircase inside it led now to a vast hall. A throne stood at the center, fashioned from overlapping scales. Behind it, a folded wing crossed the darkness like a ridge of mountains.

Something slept on the throne. Its head was hidden by a veil of pale sand, but its breath moved the empty chair at its feet. The young man saw the painted hand resting on that chair, holding a white flower.

The old woman said the sleeper had once been called a king. Before that, it had been called a storm. Before that, nobody had remained awake long enough to name it.

She brought a bowl of water and placed it beside the mirror. The sleeper's reflection appeared in the bowl, though its face remained concealed in the glass. Its closed eye contained the entire market beneath a clear, unfamiliar sky.

## An Inscription That Faced the Wall

On the reverse of the portrait, beneath a layer of dust, appeared four groups of letters:

*Khaur — vyn — zeth — omrael.*

The young man read them without moving his lips. The hand inside the painting closed around the flower. In the mirror, the sleeper lifted one claw from the throne and placed it against the red stone floor.

The market became quiet. Every salt mirror beneath its linen turned toward the same place, although none had been touched. The orange flower beneath the smallest frame released a fragrance the young man remembered from his childhood garden.

He placed one finger against the painted hand. The canvas felt warm. Somewhere behind the wall of his memory, the waiting footsteps finally moved away.

## What Was Taken Home

The old woman covered the mirror. It became light enough to hold in one hand. She wrapped it in the carpet and returned the portrait to its owner, refusing every coin he offered.

On the journey home, he did not look inside either parcel. The desert was cold, and the blue cloth above the entrance had become white. Its shadow pointed toward a road he had never noticed before.

At home, he hung the portrait in its former place. The chair remained empty, but the painted window stood open. Beyond it grew a tree heavy with oranges.

He kept the mirror covered. Sometimes, while passing it at night, he heard the slow shifting of sand over an enormous folded wing.

By morning, a single white flower would be lying beneath the frame.
